Fiido C21 tail light

Salvaged red LED strip (original driver lost), driven from the display's light output by an Arduino Pro Mini.

Strip

  • Red LEDs, all in parallel. Forward voltage ~2.0 V.
  • Runs at 300 mA (0.6 W). Measured; gives the original light's brightness.
  • Never connect it straight to a voltage source. At 5 V unlimited it drew 5 A and damaged the LEDs.

Bike wiring

  • Taps the display's light output, so the tail light follows the front light and draws nothing when parked.
  • Red = negative, green = positive (~40 V; 36 V pack, 42 V full). Yellow = horn.
  • Adds ~0.05 A to the display output.

Gotchas

  • Don't use the buck's current limit instead of the resistor. It would limit the Pro Mini's supply too.
  • IRL3103 gate and drain are easy to swap. Left = gate (pin 9 via 220 Ω), middle = drain (strip −). Swapped, the strip stays dark and pin 9 drives into the MOSFET.
  • Insulate the MOSFET tab if it can touch the strip's heatsink or the frame.
  • Bond the strip to aluminium. It has no housing to shed heat.

Flashing

  • FTDI adapter on the 6-pin header, BLK to BLK. Bike supply off: the adapter's VCC powers the rail and the strip.
  • Press reset on the Pro Mini when "Uploading" appears. This adapter doesn't auto-reset the board; without it the upload fails with not in sync: resp=0x00.
  • If the adapter drops off USB mid-upload, the strip's 300 mA is browning it out: power the rail from the buck and leave the adapter's VCC unconnected.

Light pattern

taillight/taillight.ino, PWM on D9. The toggle switch on D2 picks the mode.

  • Switch open: slow pulses, 2.8 s each (PULSE_MS). Never below 25% brightness, so the light is always visible.
  • Switch closed: steady full brightness.
  • No saved state. The lever holds the mode, so it survives the display cutting power.
  • The onboard LED (D13) blinks along with the pulses, so you can tell the sketch is running with the strip unplugged.
  • Pulsing rear lights aren't legal everywhere (e.g. Germany, StVZO). Use steady mode there.

Open items

  • Confirm the buck chip reads LM2596HVS and its input cap is ≥ 63 V. Otherwise use an XL7015 (5–80 V).
  • Check which of red/green the display switches. If it switches the negative, this circuit's GND isn't frame ground.
  • First power-up: ~3.0 V across the 10 Ω at full brightness confirms ~300 mA.
  • Bench-tested: pulsing and the mode switch work.
